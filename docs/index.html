Prusa Connect Card: mockups

An interactive mockup of a Home Assistant card for the Prusa Connect (on-premise) integration. The card is Home Assistant's own tile card, wrapped, the same way as the xTool, SVS and Lampster cards. The printer, its temperatures and its job are simulated at 10 times real speed. Nothing here talks to a printer.

On a dashboard

Tap the title row to expand or collapse the card. Set the state to Printing to watch it expand on its own, and to Finished or Stopped to watch it collapse.

Every model, collapsed

Each model gets its own picture and colors. Tap one to show it above and in the editor.

  • The progress edge runs around the card while a job prints.
  • The ring and badge on the picture show the state, with an icon as well as a color.
  • Print controls sit beside the title when they are the first feature, as with xTool.

In the editor

The editor is Home Assistant's own tile card editor, wrapped, with a printer picker above it and a Prusa section below it. Edit a feature to choose its controls: add any of them, remove any of them (at least one stays), and drag the handles to sort them. The preview follows every change.

Card configuration (YAML) 
Options at their default value are left out, as in the xTool card.

Each style paints the card's body and its title window. A new card gets its model's style, and any style can be picked for any printer. These colors are first proposals, to be checked against Prusa's product photos before release.

  


  
Decisions to review

  
Reply with a number to comment on any of these.

  
    
    
  1. Feature grouping. Job, Print controls, Temperatures, Tools, Motion and Camera follow the printer page in Prusa Connect: the status and job, the temperatures, the tools and filament, the movement and speed, and the cameras. The grouping will be checked against the on-premise web interface before the card is built.
    2. 
    
  2. The temperature graph. In the real card, Home Assistant's own chart element (the one its history and statistics cards use) draws the graph, fed from Home Assistant's history. The card only chooses the series, their colors, and dashed lines for targets. The drawing here is an approximation of that element.
    3. 
    
  3. Target controls. Each target is Home Assistant's own numeric-input card feature, bound to the integration's number entity (0 turns a heater off). Preheat sets the nozzle and bed targets from Prusa's material presets, and Cooldown sets every target to 0.
    4. 
    
  4. Chamber. The integration does not report the chamber yet. The card looks for a chamber temperature sensor and a chamber target number with the same naming as the bed, and shows the chamber only when they exist. The CORE One here shows the chamber as if they did.
    5. 
    
  5. XL toolheads. Today the integration reports a nozzle target only for the first toolhead of the XL. The other toolheads' targets may show as unknown until it reports them all.
    6. 
    
  6. Print controls. Pause and Resume share one key, which follows the state. Stop asks for a second tap to confirm.
    7. 
    
  7. Expanding and collapsing. The card expands when a job starts and collapses when it finishes or is stopped. Only the change of state does this, so the card does not fight you when you tap it open or closed.
    8. 
    
  8. Camera crop. The crop, rotation and flips are stored in the card's configuration and applied to Home Assistant's own camera picture, so the integration needs no extra entities. Full frame shows the whole picture for a moment, like the xTool card's crop key.
    9. 
    
  9. MINI+. It is shown without a camera, so the Camera feature is not offered for it. With a camera on Prusa Connect, it would get the same Camera feature.
