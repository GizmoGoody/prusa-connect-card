Prusa Connect Card: mockups

An interactive mockup of a Home Assistant card for the Prusa Connect (on-premise) integration. It is Home Assistant's tile card, wrapped, and it follows the xTool card's design: the state picture and its badges, the progress edge, the styles, the features in two columns, and the editor. The printer is simulated at 10 times real speed. Nothing here talks to a printer.

On a dashboard

Tap the card to expand or collapse it. Set the state to Printing to watch it expand on its own, and to Finished or Stopped to watch it collapse. On the camera, turn on Crop and tap two corners of the picture.

Every model, collapsed

Each in its model's style. Tap one to show it above and in the editor.

In the editor

A printer picker, Home Assistant's own tile card editor, and a Prusa section, as in the xTool card's editor. Edit a feature to choose its controls: drag the chips to sort them, remove any but the last, and add more.

Styles

  
A style paints the card in the colors of the printer: its body behind the features and its window behind the title. A new card gets its model's style. None follows your theme. The colors are first proposals, to be checked against Prusa's product photos.

  


  
Decisions to review

  
Reply with a number to comment on any of these.

  
    
    
  1. Feature grouping. Print controls, Job, Temperatures, Tools, Motion and Camera follow the printer page in Prusa Connect. It will be checked against the on-premise web interface before the card is built.
    2. 
    
  2. The state picture. It is the state's icon on a circle tinted in the state's color, as the xTool card draws its picture. Printing moves gently, Idle breathes, Attention blinks, and none of them move with reduced motion turned on.
    3. 
    
  3. Badges. The status badge (top right) shows the first that applies of: a heater on while the printer is not printing, and an update to the Prusa Connect server. The connection badge (bottom right) shows the integration's Connectivity sensor, or that the printer is unavailable.
    4. 
    
  4. No status light. The xTool card's ring copies the laser's own status light. Prusa printers report no light, so the card has no ring.
    5. 
    
  5. The temperature graph. Home Assistant's own chart element draws it in the real card, fed from Home Assistant's history. The card chooses the series, their colors and dashed targets. It sits on a dark screen like the camera, so its colors read the same on every style.
    6. 
    
  6. Targets. Each heater is a read-out of its temperature, a field for its target (a pane of the window's glass), and a plus-minus key: press it and slide, and the target changes faster the farther you slide, as in the xTool card. 0 turns a heater off.
    7. 
    
  7. Crop. The Crop key works as in the xTool card: turn it on, tap a corner, then the opposite corner. Turn it off to see the whole picture. The printer camera has no crop of its own, so the card keeps the crop: per Home Assistant user, in Home Assistant's own user data, so it follows you from device to device.
    8. 
    
  8. Chamber. The integration does not report the chamber yet. The card looks for a chamber temperature sensor and a chamber target number named like the bed's, and shows the chamber only when they exist. The CORE One here shows the chamber as if they did.
    9. 
    
  9. State content. As in the xTool card, the job's progress, time remaining and finish time become attributes of the State sensor, so the tile card's own State content can show them beside the state.
    10. 
    
  10. XL toolheads. Today the integration reports a nozzle target only for the first toolhead of the XL. The others may show as unknown until it reports them all.
