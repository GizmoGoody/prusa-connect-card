Prusa Connect Card: mockups

An interactive mockup of a Home Assistant card for the Prusa Connect (on-premise) integration. It is Home Assistant's tile card, wrapped, and it follows the xTool card's design. Each feature is a section of the printer's page in Prusa Connect. The printer is simulated at 10 times real speed. Nothing here talks to a printer.

On a dashboard

Tap the card to expand or collapse it. Set the state to Printing to watch it expand on its own, and to Finished or Stopped to watch it collapse. Press a target field and slide to change it, or tap it to type. On the camera, turn on Crop and tap two corners of the picture. Switch Theme shape to Rounded to see the card take a theme's corners.

Every model, collapsed

Each in its model's style. Tap one to show it above and in the editor.

Badges

Every badge the card can show, from what the integration reports. The status badge sits at the top right of the picture and shows the first chosen status that applies. The connection badge sits at the bottom right.

In the editor

The printer picker and the Prusa section are the card's. Everything inside the dashed box is Home Assistant's own tile card editor, unchanged, shown only so you can see where the card's parts sit: its Features list opens each feature's editor. Edit a feature to choose its controls: drag the chips to sort them, remove any but the last, and add more.

Styles

  
One style for each printer family, grouped and named as PrusaSlicer 2.9's configuration wizard groups them, in alphabetical order. A style has a frame, behind the features. Only the CORE family has a window, behind the title, drawn as the xTool card draws its windows: dark glass with Prusa orange glowing through and reflections across it (Randomize reflections in the editor draws them in a new pattern). The other families show the theme's own card where the window would be. The editor can replace the frame and window colors. A new card gets its printer's family. None follows your theme. The Pro HT90 is not in the PrusaSlicer wizard, so it has a style of its own for now.

  


  
Notes

  
    
    
  1. Features. Prusa print (Now, Current print), Prusa temperatures (Controls, Temperatures and Chamber), Prusa nozzles (Controls, Nozzles), Prusa movement (Controls, Coordinates and Speed), Prusa cameras (the Cameras tab) and Prusa history (the History tab, Measurements).
    2. 
    
  2. State content. As in the xTool card, the Print sensors (progress, time remaining, print time, finish time and file) become attributes of the State sensor, so Home Assistant's own State content can show any of them beside the state.
    3. 
    
  3. Layout. As in the xTool card: each feature takes one column. A camera with Full width takes both, and so does the history with Full width (off by default); a camera without it takes one column, as tall as its picture, with the next features beside it. With Last feature full width, a feature alone at the end of its row takes both. The title row keeps the same padding open or closed, and on a style, the window's edge sits as far from the first control below it as two rows of controls sit from each other.
    4. 
    
  4. Prusa history. In the real card this is Home Assistant's history graph card, embedded. The card hands it the temperature entities and sets its line colors to Prusa Connect's chart palette, in Prusa Connect's order: each reading, then its target. Each heater keeps one color, its target the same color at half strength. Home Assistant's graph takes its line colors from the theme's graph color variables, and the card sets those itself, so it can give a target its reading's color, dimmed. Its legend hides and shows lines, and its tooltip follows each entity's precision.
    5. 
    
  5. Heater readings. As in Prusa Connect: within 2 °C of the target, the plain text color; below it, the warning tint, an up arrow and a slow pulse; above it, the info tint and a down arrow. With no target, a heater above 40 °C is cooling, and below it is off, with Off under the number. A chamber with no target is off.
    6. 
    
  6. Precision. Every number shows as Home Assistant formats its entity: the precision set on the entity, or the integration's default (one decimal for temperatures, two for the nozzle diameter). Fan speeds and the nozzle diameter show without units. Timers show seconds only under a minute.
    7. 
    
  7. Left out for now. The MMU entities (slots and feeder), until they can be checked on a printer that has one.
    8. 
    
  8. Crop. The Crop key works as in the xTool card. The printer camera has no crop of its own, so the card keeps the crop in memory for as long as the page is open. Turning Crop off shows the whole picture.
    9. 
    
  9. Only Z while printing. Prusa movement shows only Z while a print runs, since X and Y change too fast to read. The integration turns the axis entities off by default; enable them to see them here.
    10. 
    
  10. Chamber. The integration does not report the chamber yet. The card looks for a chamber temperature and a chamber target named like the bed's, and the CORE One here shows them as if they existed.
